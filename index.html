About the course

This is the first statistics course most psychology majors take in university. It introduces the basic principles behind statistical analyses in psychological research and prepares you for more advanced courses.

We cover the types of variables used in psychological research, statistical and graphical ways to summarize them, two-variable correlation, and comparing means. Null hypothesis significance testing (NHST) is introduced, but the focus is on understanding relationships among variables, including effect sizes and confidence intervals. You will analyze data in the open-source software R.

WhyPsychology increasingly asks how large and meaningful an effect is, not just whether it’s statistically significant. You’ll learn both, and why the difference matters.

WhyR is free, and it’s a skill valued in graduate programs, industry, and government. It’s worth putting on your résumé.

Required text (free)

Cote, L. R., Gordon, R. G., Randell, C. E., Schmitt, J., & Marvin, H. Introduction to statistics in the psychological sciences. Open Textbook Library.

WhyI assign free materials so cost never determines who can keep up.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, you should be able to:

  1. Compute descriptive and inferential statistics.
  2. Interpret and report the results of descriptive and inferential statistics.
  3. Distinguish between the roles of descriptive and inferential statistics.

Topics

  • Key statistical terms
  • Frequency distributions
  • Central tendency
  • Variability
  • z-scores and the normal distribution
  • Probability
  • Sampling distributions
  • Confidence intervals
  • Power
  • Effect sizes
  • Correlation (Pearson and alternatives)
  • Hypothesis testing: independent and dependent designs
  • One-sample and two-sample t-tests
  • χ² goodness of fit
  • χ² test of independence

Effect sizes are taught alongside every inferential test in the course.

How the class works

The course combines asynchronous and in-person components. Before each class, you read the assigned chapter(s) and watch the matching pre-recorded lecture on eClass. Class time is then spent applying what you learned: brief lecture, live polling, and learning activities done by hand calculation and coding in R, with support and feedback from the instructor and teaching assistant.

Before class

  • Read the assigned chapter(s)
  • Watch the pre-recorded lecture on eClass

In class

  • Answer live Vevox polls
  • Work through hand calculations and R activities
  • Get feedback in the moment from the instructor and TA

If you miss a class, review that week's material and activities on your own so you get the same learning experience. All course materials and announcements are on eClass. A laptop or phone is helpful in class for the activities.

WhyWatching lectures on your own time means class can focus on understanding rather than keeping up: working through problems and getting feedback while you’re stuck, not after.

WhyVevox responses are anonymous, so you can commit to an answer without worrying about being wrong in front of others. The class results show me right away which ideas need another look.

Tests

Tests draw on the textbook, lectures and recordings, and include theory, application, and interpreting R input and output. You will not need to write R code on tests. A basic or scientific calculator is allowed (not a phone or tablet).

  • Test 1: research design, descriptive statistics, visualizing data, sampling/estimation and probability (Ch. 1–5)
  • Test 2: hypothesis testing and comparing means (Ch. 6–10)
  • Test 3: cumulative, with a focus on correlations and frequency analysis (Ch. 12 and 14)

Assignments

Two assignments ask you to analyze a dataset in R and write up the results. Each is handed out one week before it is due.

WhyEach assignment asks you to apply what you’ve learned to a new problem, the way researchers do, and ends with a short reflection on what you did.

Engagement points

You earn engagement points by answering the in-class Vevox polls each week.

Two freebies. Everyone can miss two classes' worth of engagement points with no grade penalty, no questions asked. Because this flexibility is built in, other missed engagement points are excused only in truly exceptional circumstances.

WhyLife happens: work, caregiving, illness. The two freebies mean you never have to explain your circumstances or lose marks for missing a class.

Generative AI

Generative AI is permitted on assignments, with parameters. If you use AI, you must:

  • fact-check all AI output;
  • critically evaluate AI-generated content and integrate it properly with your own ideas;
  • attach a reflection (minimum 150 words) stating which tool you used, how you used it (e.g., generating ideas, explaining concepts, summarizing), and what it could and could not help you with.

WhyAI is becoming part of how research and professional work get done, so rather than ban it, I want you to learn to use it well: check what it gives you, and notice what it can’t do. The reflection is where you practise that.

AI tools and all other electronic devices are not permitted during tests.

Course policies

Missed tests

All tests, including make-ups, are written in person. If you miss a test due to illness, email the TA within 48 hours to schedule a make-up (the format may differ). You must also complete the Psychology department's Missed Tests/Exams Form; not doing so may result in a grade of zero.

Late assignments

Late assignments lose 10% of the assignment's value per weekday late. The Missed Tests/Exams Form also applies to late assignments.

Academic consideration

Under York's Policy on Academic Consideration for Missed Course Work, you may submit up to two self-reported absences per 12-week term through eClass, without a doctor's note, for work worth 20% or less. Each covers up to seven consecutive days. Contact me within two business days after the absence period ends.

Accessibility

Students with disabilities are encouraged to register with Student Accessibility Services as early as possible. Please also let me know early in the term if you anticipate needing accommodation, so we can plan how to meet your needs within the course.

Academic integrity

All submitted work must be your own. Sharing test answers (including in group chats such as Discord or WhatsApp), posting test questions to homework-help sites, contract cheating, unauthorized collaboration, and buying or selling test banks are breaches of York's Academic Conduct Policy. If you're unsure whether something is allowed, ask me or the TA.

Devices during tests

No electronic devices except a basic or scientific calculator. Phones must be turned off and stored in your bag under your chair.

Grading

Grades follow York's 9-point grading system (e.g., A+ = 90–100, A = 80–89, B+ = 75–79).
